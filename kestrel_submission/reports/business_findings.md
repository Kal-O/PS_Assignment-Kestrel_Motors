# Kestrel Motor Group — Business Findings

**Reporting Period:** October 2025 – September 2026

## 1. Customer Experience Trends

Analysis of the consolidated customer feedback data indicates that the negative rating rate increased during the second half of the reporting period.

### 1.1 Comparison Between Reporting Periods

- **October 2025 – March 2026:** 19.8% negative rating rate (669 of 3,382 rated responses).
- **April 2026 – September 2026:** 24.6% negative rating rate (919 of 3,733 rated responses).
- **Overall increase:** Approximately 4.8 percentage points.

The highest monthly negative rating rates occurred in:

- **August 2026:** 26.54%
- **June 2026:** 26.51%
- **April 2026:** 26.32%

### 1.2 Business Interpretation

These results suggest a deterioration in reported customer satisfaction during the second half of the reporting period.

However, changes in dealership participation, rating scales, response composition and data collection methods may influence these results. A like-for-like dealership comparison is recommended before attributing the increase solely to operational performance.

### 1.3 Preliminary Recommendation

Investigate operational changes and recurring customer complaints associated with the months that recorded higher negative rating rates, particularly within Service departments.

## 2. Department Performance

Service recorded the highest negative rating rate among the three main departments, making it the primary area for further investigation.

| Department | Negative Ratings | Total Rated Responses | Negative Rating Rate |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| Service | 1,284 | 4,790 | 26.81% |
| Parts | 111 | 689 | 16.11% |
| Sales | 165 | 1,524 | 10.83% |

### Business Interpretation

Service has a substantially higher negative rating rate than either Parts or Sales. This indicates that Service should be the initial focus of operational investigation and customer experience improvement initiatives.

Further analysis should examine whether recurring issues relate to communication, waiting times, pricing, billing transparency, parts availability, repair quality or staff courtesy.

These potential drivers require validation against actual customer comments and operational records before conclusions are drawn.

## 3. Priority Dealerships

### 3.1 Recommended Initial Pilot: Chermside (QLD01)

Chermside is recommended as an initial dealership for a customer experience improvement pilot.

Key indicators include:

- **Negative Service rating rate:** 27.71%.
- **Negative Service ratings:** 87.
- **Total rated Service responses:** 314.
- **Annual Service revenue:** Approximately A$5.34 million.

Chermside's negative Service rating rate, combined with its commercial significance, makes it a relevant candidate for investigating potential service improvements.

### 3.2 Additional Dealerships for Investigation

**Ringwood and Townsville** are additional candidates for investigation because of their substantial Service volumes and revenue exposure.

The final prioritisation should consider:

- The frequency and nature of customer complaints.
- Consistency of performance over time.
- Service volume and revenue exposure.
- Operational feasibility and available resources.
- The potential to measure improvement against a reliable baseline.

### Business Recommendation

Begin with Chermside as a pilot location, while investigating Ringwood and Townsville to determine whether similar customer experience issues occur across higher-volume dealerships.
